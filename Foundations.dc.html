Job Triage · design system

Foundations

Green, red and white. Every colour in the app resolves through a name below, so the two themes are one design at two lightnesses rather than two designs. Nothing outside the two token blocks hardcodes a colour — if you are adding UI, take a token or add one here first.

What the colours mean

Three hues, four jobs

The palette carries meaning, not decoration. Before picking a colour, decide which of these you are saying.

GREEN
The primary action, and a posted role. The ordinary good thing.
RED
Overdue, destructive, wrong. The only colour permitted to interrupt.
DESATURATED
A cold approach. Nobody has answered yet, so it is cool and quiet.
WHITE
A live conversation. Somebody is there, so it is the brightest thing on screen.

Temperature is encoded as lightness, and that reading inverts under the light theme: --live becomes the deepest green on a white page rather than the palest tone. Reachability stays grey on purpose — it is a second axis, not a verdict, and must never compete with the channel colours for attention.

Type

Two families, one ramp

--disp is system-ui for everything readable. --mono is ui-monospace and is reserved for numbers and machine facts — scores, dates, counts, labels the app generated rather than a person wrote. That split is the strongest signal in the type system: if a human wrote it, it is not mono.

Metrics

Radii, borders, spacing, shadow

Small radii and 1px borders throughout — this is a dense working tool, not a marketing page. Nothing is rounded past 6px, and no border is thicker than 1px except a card's left accent, which is carrying meaning.

Rules worth keeping
  • Group siblings with flex + gap, never margins or source whitespace.
  • Numbers are --mono with tabular-nums, so columns of them line up.
  • Motion is one 0.45s rise on entry; everything else is 0.12–0.22s. All of it is off under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • No emoji. Icons are inline SVG, stroke-based, on a 24px grid.
